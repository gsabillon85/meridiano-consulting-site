Where we go deep

Six practices, one discipline underneath. We build the model, translate it into a business rule, put it into production and stay to monitor it — which is where most analytics work quietly stops.

Modelling

The core of the practice. Two doctorates' worth of method, pointed at problems where the wrong model costs real money.

Credit modelling across the full lifecycle

From application through to mature customers, across cards, accounts, fraud, servicing, CRM and growth. Each stage has a different question and a different cost of error.

Survival and reliability analysis

Predicting when an event happens, not just whether it will — the customer about to churn, the loan about to default, the machine about to fail.

Advanced linear and non-linear models

When the relationship is not a straight line, forcing it into one is the most expensive shortcut in analytics.

Oil and gas modelling

Sector-specific work where physical constraints and long horizons change what a model can honestly claim.

Advanced statistical analysis

Multivariate, univariate, probabilistic and inferential methods; time series and financial series; quality control, risk and finance; health and environmental statistics. The technique follows the question, never the other way round.

Financial markets

End to end, not a slice. We have worked every stage of the credit cycle, which means we know how a decision made at acquisition surfaces as a loss two years later.

The full credit cycle

Acquisition, underwriting, portfolio management, risk operations and collections. Most consultancies specialise in one link of that chain. The expensive mistakes happen between the links — a scorecard tuned for approval rates that quietly wrecks recovery, a collections policy that contradicts the risk appetite it was meant to serve.

Hard problems

The cases where standard tooling fails and someone has to know the statistics well enough to build around the failure.

Recommendation systems

Matching at scale, where relevance and business objective are not the same thing.

Causal inference

Separating what actually caused the outcome from what merely moved alongside it. Correlation-driven decisions are the costliest kind.

Reject inference

Learning from the applications you turned down — a blind spot in every credit portfolio that was never corrected for it.

Severe class imbalance

Fraud, rare defaults, uncommon diagnoses. When one class is 0.1% of the data, accuracy becomes a meaningless metric.

High-volume data

Datasets where the method has to be chosen for what will actually run, not only for what is statistically ideal.

High-dimensional data

Thousands of candidate variables. Selecting the ones that matter is precisely the problem our doctoral research addressed.

Business impact

A model that nobody acts on has no value. This is the translation layer, and it is where most technical projects fail.

Applied, not theoretical

Every method we use has been run in a live commercial operation, under real constraints and real deadlines.

Technical indicators into business value

An AUC of 0.82 means nothing in a board meeting. Turning that into currency is a skill in itself.

Impact calculation

What the change is actually worth, stated before the project starts and measured after it ships.

Experimentation

A/B, A/B/C and multi-arm designs — so the gain is demonstrated rather than asserted.

Business rules from statistical models

Turning a score into a policy your operation can execute, audit and defend to a regulator.

Continuous monitoring

Models drift. Without monitoring, the question is not whether performance degrades but when you notice.

Data engineering

The unglamorous layer that decides whether any of the above is possible. We build it ourselves rather than waiting on a queue.

Public data acquisition

Via connection or crawling, to enrich what you already hold with context you do not.

Ingestion and layered processing

Raw through to curated, with each layer doing one job and doing it traceably.

Query optimisation and table design

Cutting cost and latency, and building the tables the analysis actually needs instead of working around the ones that exist.

Data linkage

Joining records across sources with no shared key — a hard statistical problem disguised as a plumbing one.

Data loss detection and remediation

Finding the gaps before they reach the model. Missing data is rarely missing at random, and treating it as though it were is one of the quietest ways to build a biased system.

Deployment

We ship. The handover is a running system inside your operation, not a notebook and a slide deck.

End-to-end solutions

From data source to the screen where someone makes the decision.

Enabling the operation

Training and process design so your team runs the solution after we leave.

Integration and APIs

Connecting to the systems you already run, rather than asking the business to adapt to the model.

Latency, caching and evictions

A prediction that arrives after the decision was made is worth nothing. Performance engineering is part of the statistics.

Containers and infrastructure

Reproducible environments, so the model behaves in production exactly as it did in testing.

General software engineering

Version control, testing and documentation. The parts that decide whether the system still works in two years.

How an engagement runs

The same five steps on every project, from the smallest to the largest. You know from day one what you receive, when you receive it and what it costs.

1

Intro call

Around 40 minutes, no charge. We map the problem and tell you plainly whether we are the right firm for it.

2

Discovery

Two to four weeks listening to your team and reading your numbers. It ends with the causes identified and the options on the table.

3

Solution design

We build the alternatives with your team and weigh impact against risk. The decision is reached together, not handed to you finished.

4

Implementation

We stay through execution with a fixed meeting rhythm and a live progress view in your client portal.

5

Measurement

Ninety days later we come back and measure the result against what was promised. Without that step, the project is not finished.

Who you actually work with

Two statisticians with graduate degrees from USP and UFSCar, and a partner with a career in healthcare, working across Brazil, Panama and Honduras. The partnership is small by design: every engagement is led by a partner, from first diagnosis to final delivery.

Gustavo Sabillón

Founding partner · Statistical modelling

PhD (2024) and MSc (2020) in Statistics from the joint programme of the University of São Paulo and the Federal University of São Carlos. His research covered variable selection in hidden Markov models — in plain terms, how to tell that a system has changed behaviour and which variables explain the change. That is the difference between spotting a decline after the quarter closes and spotting it while there is still time to act.

Eight years applying that work outside academia, on operational and credit data. Honduran; runs engagements in English, Spanish and Portuguese.

Jadson Marcelino

More than ten years in credit, retail and manufacturing, building risk models and decision policies.
